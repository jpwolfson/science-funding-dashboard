How to read this
Oct–Jul basis. Fiscal years run Oct–Sep. Comparisons use the Oct–Jul window so a fiscal year in progress can be compared like-for-like with completed years, without the heavy August–September award season distorting it.
The dollar series can be outlier-driven. Institute- or center-scale awards can be a large share of a year's dollars in the top three awards alone, so the stacked dollars chart separates the top 3 from the rest — year-over-year swings in the total can track a handful of large awards rather than the broader research-grant base.
Award mechanisms differ by agency. NSF reports standard and continuing grants; NIH reports annual application records that this site groups as new/competing, noncompeting continuation, fellowship, and other awards. Interpret the mechanism chart before reading a headline count change as a funding cut.
Amendment bias. Reported award totals are read as currently stated, including later upward amendments. Older cohorts have had longer to accumulate them, which biases recent years low by an unmeasured amount.
Pagination fault, handled. The NSF API can silently drop or duplicate records on large paginated queries. The pull adapters keep every query window small (bisecting as needed) and cross-check against independently verified baselines. Data refreshes weekly via GitHub Actions; award-level CSVs live on each division's page.
